Focus, a case study

Focus is a focus timer for the robot, in the style of the Pomodoro Technique®: focus for 25 minutes, a 5 minute break, and a longer break after every four. The robot is the timer; a phone is optional. Its code: {{.Repo}} at {{.Tag}} (Apache-2.0), online at focus.q41.eu. What the robot and the phone show

  • The screen: the phase (FOCUS, SHORT BREAK, LONG BREAK), the time left as a ring, today's count with the daily goal, the set's dots, and a line for the room: FOCUSING, STRETCH, TAKE A WALK, PAUSED, TAP TO FOCUS.
  • The LEDs: both strips show the time left in the same six steps, dim: red while focusing, green or blue on a break, amber when paused, off when idle. A sign for others in the room.
  • Touch: tap the screen, or touch the head, to start or pause; hold the screen to skip to the next phase.
  • The end of a phase: a rising bell and a nod after focus; two calm notes and a look up after a break.
  • The phone or computer (paired by the robot's QR code, or signed in through the manager; optional): the same timer with Start/Pause, Skip and Reset, what you focus on, marks for interruptions, today's focus periods, days in a row, the last seven days, and the settings.

The key code

The robots, through the kit's hub

The hub checks the robots' tokens (the shared one, or one a manager gave the robot), keeps one connection per robot, makes the pairing codes for its QR screen and reports the paired browsers to the manager. Focus only says what to do when a robot connects, leaves or reports something; the timer is its own.

No browser needed

A robot whose app has no paired browser shows its QR screen. The timer needs no browser, so the robot always hears of at least one, and shows the timer at once (the QR code stays a swipe away).

{{template "excerpt" (excerpt "focus/paired")}}

Touches: one action each

The robot reports raw input: a touch on the screen, a press on the head. Focus starts or pauses on either, at most once in 1.5 s (touchGap), so one touch is one action however it is reported; a long press skips.

{{template "excerpt" (excerpt "focus/touch")}}

Send only what changed

The robot's picture is Focus's own drawing, sent as a JPEG. A key names each picture, and a new one goes to the robot only when the key changes: six times a phase when calm, every second only when the seconds are shown. The LEDs likewise.

The end of a phase

The chimes are the kit's (sound.Chime), each short enough for one speaker message (the robot buffers about 3 s); the head nods or looks up, unless the person turned the moves off.

The rules, pure

The timer never reads a clock: the app's loop calls Tick with the time every 200 ms, and the tests call it with any time they like, so a whole set of focus periods and breaks runs in milliseconds.

What it teaches

  • Let the kit hold the robots. Tokens, connections, pairing codes and the manager's reports are twenty lines of configuration (newHub); the app keeps its own state.
  • Keep the rules pure. A model that takes the time from its caller is easy to test, and the robot, the page and the statistics all read the same timer.
  • The screen is a picture you draw. Any Go image becomes the robot's screen; the kit's screen draws cards and questions the same way (Dev's show).
  • Send what changed, not every tick. A key per picture and the last LEDs keep the robot's connection quiet and its screen calm.
  • Work without a phone. Telling the robot it has a viewer puts the app on its screen from the start; the phone adds settings and statistics.
  • One action per touch. A short gap turns a touch that is reported twice into one start or pause.
  • Test end to end. A fake robot on a real connection and a test clock run the app as the robot would (app_test.go).

Also in the kit for an app like it: leds.Bar and leds.Dim make a bar like Focus's own LEDs, robot.Play paces a sound of any length for the speaker, and robot.Gesture and robot.Look check that the robot offers the command first.
